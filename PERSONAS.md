# Personas: BioComm Copilot

---

### Persona 1 — The Diligence Analyst

**Name:** Maya Chen
**Title:** Business Development Analyst
**Company:** Series A biotech, 20-person team, UC/GI focus
**Experience:** 2 years in biotech BD, background in biology research

**Context:**
Maya is the person who actually does the work. When a new asset comes across the desk, it lands on her to build the first-pass memo before anyone senior looks at it. She's evaluating 3–5 assets at any given time, each at a different stage of diligence.

**Goals:**
- Produce a complete, credible first-pass memo without spending two days on it
- Not get embarrassed by missing an obvious competitor or citing a wrong trial status
- Hand something to her VP that makes her look thorough, not sloppy

**Current workflow:**
PubMed → ClinicalTrials.gov → company websites → SEC EDGAR → internal knowledge → PowerPoint. Three tabs open at once. Notes in a Google Doc. Two to three days per memo on average.

**Pain points:**
- Spends most of her time finding and organizing information, not thinking about it
- Inconsistent memo quality depending on how much time she had
- Afraid of missing something important that a senior person catches later
- Has to re-research things she's researched before because nothing is organized

**What she needs from BioComm Copilot:**
A reliable first draft she can edit, not a blank page she has to fill. She needs to trust that the sources are real, the competitors are complete, and the gaps are flagged before she sends anything up the chain.

**Trust breakers:**
A fabricated trial ID. A missing obvious competitor. A valuation stated as fact.

### Persona 2 — The Head of BD

**Name:** David Park
**Title:** Vice President, Business Development
**Company:** Series B biotech, 45-person team
**Experience:** 12 years in pharma and biotech licensing

**Context:**
David doesn't build memos — he reviews them and decides which assets go to the next stage of diligence. He's evaluating 10–15 assets per quarter across multiple indications. He relies on his analysts for the first pass, but he's the one who gets on the call with the counterparty and eventually signs the term sheet.

**Goals:**
- Quickly triage which assets deserve deeper diligence vs. a pass
- Trust that the memo his analyst produced didn't miss anything obvious
- Make faster initial decisions without compromising rigor

**Current workflow:**
Reads the memo his analyst builds. Asks follow-up questions. Sends back for revisions. Repeats.

**Pain points:**
- First-pass memos are inconsistent — quality depends on which analyst built them and how much time they had
- Spends time correcting or re-researching things the memo got wrong or missed
- The time from "asset arrives" to "we have a real view" is too long

**What he needs from BioComm Copilot:**
The Decision Summary. He wants to scan commercial opportunity, confidence score, key risks, and recommended next step in under 60 seconds. If the signal is interesting, he drills into the full memo. If not, he passes. Speed of triage is the value.

**Trust breakers:**
Any obvious competitor missing from the landscape. Regulatory claims stated with false confidence. A comp deal that clearly isn't comparable.

### Persona 3 — The Scrappy CEO

**Name:** Priya Okafor
**Title:** CEO & Co-Founder
**Company:** Seed-stage biotech, 6-person team
**Experience:** 10 years in academic drug discovery, first time as a CEO

**Context:**
Priya doesn't have a BD team. She is the BD team. She's a scientist by training and does licensing and partnership conversations herself, often while also managing the science, the board, and the fundraise. She knows the biology cold but finds the commercial and deal structure side harder to move through quickly.

**Goals:**
- Get up to speed on the commercial landscape for her asset fast enough to have informed conversations with potential partners
- Understand what comparable deals look like before she walks into a negotiation
- Know what her regulatory path looks like without hiring a consultant for every question

**Current workflow:**
Reads whatever she can find. Calls advisors. Pays for market research reports she half-reads. Takes a lot longer than she wants.

**Pain points:**
- Commercial and deal intelligence takes time she doesn't have
- Doesn't always know what she doesn't know — misses things because she doesn't know to look for them
- Consultant fees for quick regulatory or market questions are hard to justify at her stage

**What she needs from BioComm Copilot:**
A fast, honest overview that covers the domains she's less expert in. She doesn't need it to be perfect — she needs it to be comprehensive enough that she's not walking into conversations blind. She trusts herself to interrogate the output critically once she has it.

**Trust breakers:**
Anything that confidently states something she knows to be wrong from her scientific expertise. That destroys trust in the rest of the memo immediately.

### Persona 4 — The Tech Transfer Licensing Manager

**Name:** James Whitfield
**Title:** Licensing Manager
**Company:** University tech transfer office
**Experience:** 7 years in IP licensing and university spinout commercialization

**Context:**
James evaluates early-stage academic discoveries for commercialization potential. He's assessing 20–30 assets per year, most of them preclinical or very early stage, across a wide range of therapeutic areas. He's not a deep domain expert in any single area — he needs to get oriented fast in a new space and understand whether there's a commercial path before investing time in a full evaluation.

**Goals:**
- Quickly determine whether an asset has a viable commercial path before committing resources to a full licensing evaluation
- Understand the competitive landscape well enough to position the asset for potential licensees
- Produce a briefing document that a faculty inventor or external partner can receive

**Current workflow:**
Literature search, competitive scan, maybe a conversation with the faculty inventor, and an internal one-page summary. Often shallow because of volume.

**Pain points:**
- Too many assets to evaluate deeply — most get a quick pass based on incomplete information
- Not a domain expert in most of the areas he evaluates — has to ramp up from scratch each time
- Needs to communicate commercial potential to non-commercial audiences (faculty, department heads)

**What he needs from BioComm Copilot:**
Rapid orientation to a new therapeutic area and asset class. The competitive and regulatory sections are most valuable to him. The Deal Comparables section helps him anchor conversations about licensing economics with faculty inventors who have unrealistic expectations.

**Trust breakers:**
Deal comparable valuations that look too precise or optimistic. Faculty inventors will push back hard if the numbers don't hold up.
